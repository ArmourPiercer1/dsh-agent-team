(process, agent-control, ui, agent-state, inspect) and one turned out **not** to be a
retirement unit at all. The board is now complete for every family the census books
except the test-only probe family. Nothing was switched, nothing was deleted, and no
approval was manufactured.

**How every cell is graded.** `RetireAllowed = OldBaseline ∧ NewPathVerified ∧
SemanticDifferencesResolved ∧ NegativeControlsPass` (`RETIREMENT.md` §1). A family may
retire **incrementally** and only on its own evidence; a green FS cell is not evidence
for Search, and a green Search cell is not evidence for Web (`RETIREMENT.md` §2).
`UNKNOWN`, an unobserved effect, or a skipped arm is never a PASS.

## 2. The three shapes a family can be in (and why the gate keeps them apart)

1. **Measured on both paths** (fs, search): the old baseline exists *and* every vector
   was reproduced on the new path. The only thing standing between them and retirement
   is a signature — and for search, a decision about a real product defect.
2. **Measured on the old path only** (web, process, agent-control, ui, agent-state):
   five families have a frozen, reproducible old-path corpus and real per-member
   controls, and **no new path exists at all**. Their `newPathVerified=false` is an
   absence recorded in a file (`new-path-absent.txt`), never an empty PASS transcript.
3. **Not a retirement unit** (inspect): Stage-0 measured that every booked spelling has
   exactly one implementation, so there is no second path to retire. The gate refuses it
   with `UTE_OLD_BASELINE_MISSING` **and** `UTE_UNIT_UNGRADABLE` — the second code is the
   honest one: there is no corpus to parse because there is no corpus.

## 3. What the five new families found that the first three could not

- **Most of the names are declared twice, and most of those host declarations are
  mounted.** Six of the seven ui/agent-state spellings are declared by the governed body
  *and* by a pinned host package the Member preset mounts (`present` plus all five
  agent-state rows). `ask_user_question` is the
  exception: both sides declare it, but PD-25 removes `tool-ask-user` from the preset, so
  the host implementation is not reachable. Web is a different shape again: its second
  claimant is this repo's own `dsh-agent-team/web-tool` preset row, not a pinned host
  package. Deleting the governed bodies therefore removes less than a user can reach —
  this is the round's most important finding, because a "retirement" can be performed
  perfectly and change no observable behaviour. Inspect's `alias-to-native-name-change`
  is not a collision at all: the spellings differ (an alias→native rename).
- **The product route does not mount the governed bodies.** For agent-control, the route-C
  attach registers none of the seven names (`unknown tool`, `providerCalls=0`); the
  independent proof is the preset's removed delegation group in `cordis.patch.yml`, not
  the deliberately no-preset probe. For ui/agent-state, the *bare* route registers none of
  the names, but the shipped preset serves six of the seven from host rows (`present` on
  the preset scope + all five agent-state names) while `ask_user_question` is absent by PD-25.
  What is absent is the governed body, not reachability of the names. Each lane says so in
  a named difference (B7).
- **A family can have a member its own wiring cannot reach.** `process` books `bash` and
  `pwsh`; the shipped preset **removes** the `pwsh` row and no route registers it, so the
  unit stays short at 1/2 and the exclusion is derived from the wiring (not from "this
  laptop has no pwsh" — that assertion was written, failed on GitHub's runners, and was
  replaced with a conditional law plus a control).
