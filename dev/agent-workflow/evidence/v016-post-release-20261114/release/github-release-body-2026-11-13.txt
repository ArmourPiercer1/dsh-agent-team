## What is stable
- Member × Tool two-axis ceilings enforced on both the service and the OS execution paths.
- Governed `bash` with durable `control-execution-result` facts: a **refused** execution is recorded `failed`, never `succeeded`.
- Team create → member execute → human approval → result collection, with cold recovery of durable state.
- Goal creation, active/paused lifecycle and Stop.

## What is opt-in experimental (Alpha.6 review routing, default **off**)
- Officer review of `leader-approval` legs: allow, deny, exception, Stop and crash paths measured on a real Host.
- Two decision channels: `leader-approval` → Leader or human (or the case/leg-assigned Officer when Alpha.6 is on); `user-approval` → **human only**; `envelope-mutation` → Leader or human. The ban is self-approval: the executor may never decide its own operation.
- `team_list_pending_control` is caller-scoped: the Leader sees every open case at its current leg (including `user-approval`), an assigned Officer sees its own current legs, an unassigned member is refused with zero rows. A role-granted all-requests view carrying resolver kind + identity is registered (#756), **not implemented**.

## Must not be claimed
1. `succeeded` does not mean "the command completed": a **timed-out** or shell-session-exited command is recorded `unknown`, and a signal-killed one `failed`.
2. Sticky Stop is **latch-scoped** (accepted experimental limit, PD-40): a Stop does not unconditionally stop a later, differently-latched turn.
3. After a **cold restart** the approval backend does not wake a reviewer. The measured operator procedure — Leader checks pending controls, closes what it may, messages the Officer, then messages members — was verified in 12 real-Host runs (Officer decides in 251/253 ms; without the briefing the ask starves). See the Leader skill's 「重启工作 / Restarting work」.
4. No upgrade protocol; rollback means installing a new version, never moving a tag. No npm publish, no provenance, no signed artifact. Linux only (landlock); no second machine class was measured.
